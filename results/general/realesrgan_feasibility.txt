  Original projection: measured 2026-09-15 20:07
  Correction:          measured 2026-09-15 22:37

================================================================================
CORRECTION - the projection below overstates the cost by ~2.6x
================================================================================

The projection further down was made from a single-layer Swift microbenchmark and
concludes that Real-ESRGAN-class models are far too slow to play live. That
conclusion is wrong. The same architecture family measured end-to-end through
PyTorch/MPS (scripts/realesrgan/bench.py) runs at ~7.2 TFLOP/s, not the 2.67
TFLOP/s the microbenchmark achieved, because the microbenchmark timed one layer in
isolation - paying per-dispatch overhead once against a single layer's arithmetic -
and then scaled that layer's achieved rate to a whole network.

Measured, whole network, random frames, MPS, iters=3. Full table including CPU:
results/general/realesrgan_bench.txt.

  realesr-animevideov3   (16 body convs, 0.62 M MACs/px, 621,424 params)
    480x270 -> 1920x1080     22.59 ms/frame   44.26 fps   0.68x of a 30fps budget
    640x360 -> 2560x1440     41.39 ms/frame   24.16 fps   1.24x
    854x480 -> 3416x1920     75.00 ms/frame   13.33 fps   2.25x
    960x540 -> 3840x2160     89.13 ms/frame   11.22 fps   2.67x

  realesr-general-x4v3   (32 body convs, 1.21 M MACs/px, 1,213,296 params)
    480x270 -> 1920x1080     47.58 ms/frame   21.02 fps   1.43x
    640x360 -> 2560x1440     84.12 ms/frame   11.89 fps   2.52x
    854x480 -> 3416x1920    152.54 ms/frame    6.56 fps   4.58x
    960x540 -> 3840x2160    170.46 ms/frame    5.87 fps   5.11x

Against the projection's own case - general-x4v3 at a 640x480 input, projected at
277.7 ms - the measurement brackets it at 84 ms (640x360) to 152 ms (854x480),
i.e. roughly 100-110 ms interpolated. So that projection was ~2.6x too
pessimistic, and "0.12x real time at 480p" should read as ~0.45x.

What this changes, and what it does not:

  * animevideov3 does fit the app's SD 4x case. 270p -> 1080p at 44 fps through
    PyTorch is already past 30 fps, and a hand-written Metal port would be cheaper
    again than the MPS graph. This is the one model in the Real-ESRGAN family that
    is a candidate for a live mode here, and it is the model recommended for
    anime detail.
  * general-x4v3 at 270p -> 1080p is 21 fps: near real time, not real time, and it
    is the heavier model by design (2x the MACs).
  * CPU inference is what is genuinely unplayable - 495 ms/frame at 270p and worse
    beyond - 20x slower than MPS. Any claim of the form "this model is too slow"
    is true of the CPU path and false of the GPU path.
  * x2plus and the other heavier Real-ESRGAN variants were never measured, before
    or now, and are not covered by this correction. RealBasicVSR's objection is
    unchanged and is not a throughput claim at all: it needs optical flow and a
    sequence window, so it is a batch model regardless of speed.

The "10-100x too slow to play" phrasing in docs/GENERAL-VIDEO-UPSCALING.md and
README.md is corrected to match this file.

================================================================================
ORIGINAL PROJECTION (kept as the record of what was measured and how it misled)
================================================================================

device: Apple M5 Pro
one 3x3 64->64 fp32 PReLU layer at 640x480: 8.467 ms  (2.67 TFLOP/s achieved)
general-x4v3: 1.21 M MACs per input pixel (32 body layers dominate)
projected whole network, same kernel, no allowance for pixel-shuffle or I/O:
  480p  640x480      277.7 ms/frame    3.60 fps   0.12x realtime @30fps
  SD-wide  854x480      370.5 ms/frame    2.70 fps   0.09x realtime @30fps
  540p  960x540      468.6 ms/frame    2.13 fps   0.07x realtime @30fps
  720p 1280x720      833.1 ms/frame    1.20 fps   0.04x realtime @30fps
  1080p 1920x1080    1874.4 ms/frame   0.53 fps   0.02x realtime @30fps

Why the extrapolation failed: the layer measured is one of 18-34 in the network
and ran alone, so the per-dispatch overhead was charged once to a single layer's
arithmetic; multiplying that rate by layer count then assumed a single hand-
written layer is the ceiling for the whole network, when the framework reaches
~7.2 TFLOP/s on the same arithmetic. It is a valid single-layer number and an
invalid network projection.
